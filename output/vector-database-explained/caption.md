Computers can't read meaning, but they can compare numbers. That's the trick behind vector databases.

Any piece of text can be turned into a list of numbers, called a vector, that captures what it's about. Texts with similar meaning get similar numbers. So "dog" and "puppy" sit close together, and "dog" and "invoice" sit far apart.

A vector database stores millions of these lists. Give it a new one and it returns the closest matches in a fraction of a second. That's how "cheap flights" can find "budget airfare" even though the words don't match.

You've probably used one already, maybe through a chatbot that recalls your documents. Now you know what's doing the work.
